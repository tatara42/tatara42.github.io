Seeing like an artist isn't about natural talent or drawing skill; it’s a deliberate cognitive shift in how your brain processes visual information. Normally, the brain relies on shorthand, labeling objects like a coffee mug or an eye with a mental symbol and stopping there. Artists train themselves to bypass this symbolic shortcut to observe pure visual facts: shapes, values, negative spaces, and edges. To start seeing this way, you must turn off your symbol-making brain and look at what is actually in front of you. Pay close attention to the negative space—the empty shapes surrounding and threading through objects—because mapping those empty areas accurately makes the subject fall naturally into place. Next, practice seeing in values by squinting to blur out details and reveal broad patches of light and shadow, and look past imaginary outlines to notice how objects are actually defined by where different colors and tones meet. Finally, abandon guesswork by using a pencil at arm's length to measure proportions and angles relative to one another, transforming how you visually translate the world around you.
